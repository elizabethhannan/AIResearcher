I study how large language models fail once they leave the benchmark — in the interfaces, user populations, and deployment contexts that surround them, where HXO and AXO experiences collide.

About Me

Every system I research and build is evaluated through one question: does this help or hurt the full spectrum of human experience?

Methodology: HXO and AXO ver 2.0

My research and builds run on two paired frameworks. HXO (Human Experience Optimization) measures what a system does to the people using it. AXO (Agent Experience Optimization) measures how the agents in that system behave, coordinate, and fail. Together they make up my multi-agent experience framework.

  • Define the population first. Every study names who is affected (Deaf and DeafBlind users, AAC users, early-career disabled workers, job applicants) before it names a model or a metric.
  • Test conditions, not single prompts. Each study crosses a fixed prompt corpus with interface or deployment conditions, such as 60 prompts × 6 conditions = 360 prompt–condition pairs in the sycophancy work.
  • Score both sides. HXO scores the human outcome (harm, exclusion, trust miscalibration). AXO scores the agent outcome (agreement drift, guardrail failure, hand-off errors between agents).
  • Validate across models. Findings must hold on more than one model or method before they are reported, as in the hiring study's two-model validation.
  • Ripcord (AXO): audit intervention. Every harness carries a stop rule that is written before the first run. The ripcord pulls on any one of three triggers: a single severe-harm output, a harm rate above 2% in any condition, or a gap of 10 percentage points or more between any user population and the baseline. The run halts and a two-person review follows. Each failing case is read by hand and tagged with a root cause (model, interface, or data), then either fixed and re-run or documented as a known limit. Nothing ships until both reviewers sign off, which keeps humans governing the system itself, not only the report about it.
  • Built for scale. The frameworks come from building and maintaining AI agents in production and leading a team of 50+, so each evaluation is written as a repeatable harness that can run across products and model versions rather than as a one-off test.
